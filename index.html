Part 1 · 4 minutes

First look: what is happening here?

Study the original cartoon before seeing any clues. Look carefully, but do not try to explain every symbol yet.

Historical source note: Cuba was under U.S. military occupation; Hawaii had been annexed; Puerto Rico was ceded to the U.S.; and the Philippines were claimed by the U.S. after the war with Spain. These were not identical legal situations. This 1899 cartoon uses racial caricaturesExaggerated or distorted portrayals of people; these can reinforce harmful stereotypes. and shows unequal treatment of groups. Those depictions reveal prejudices of the period; they are not accurate descriptions of the people portrayed.

Louis Dalrymple, School Begins, Puck, January 25, 1899. Library of Congress source information. Entire original image; no markers.

Part 3 · 6 minutes

Two sources, one question

Earlier today, you read Albert J. Beveridge's 1898 speech The March of the Flag. Now compare that political speechA public address intended to explain or influence views about government or policy. with Dalrymple's 1899 cartoon.

Think with your partner: Would Beveridge agree with the main argument on the cartoon's blackboard? Why? Return to your printed reading for evidence.
“Beveridge argues ___, and detail #___ in the cartoon ___.”“Although the cartoon and Beveridge both address ___, the cartoon's portrayal of ___ suggests ___.”

Whose viewpoint is missing?

Filipino revolutionariesPeople working to overthrow an existing government or political system. fought Spanish colonial rule and declared Philippine independence on June 12, 1898. Emilio Aguinaldo led the independence movement. The United States did not recognize that declaration. In 1899, fighting began between U.S. forces and Filipino independence forces. This perspective from the Filipino independence movement complicates American claims about whether Filipinos wanted or were ready for self-governmentPeople governing themselves through their own political institutions rather than by an outside power..

Part 4 · 4 minutes

Who gets to decide?

If the United States believes that government should rest on the consent of the governed, who gets to decide when that principle applies?

Discuss with a partner. You can agree with, disagree with, or qualify the arguments in the sources. Use historical evidence rather than an unsupported opinion.
